# Evermore — The Standing Order

If you were told "read PROMPT.md and continue the work": start here, then do
exactly what §5 says before building anything.

## 1. Mission

Build me my perfect MMORPG art and world.

The game is **Evermore**. The world is **Arkadion**. The work, until further
notice, is its first town: **Hearthmere** (Haven I) — a walled lake-town at a
ford, in a semi-realistic anime style (SAO, Shangri-La Frontier, Echoes of
Aincrad), at a quality a player would believe shipped in FFXIV, Guild Wars 2,
or modern World of Warcraft. The test never changes: put a render blind beside
a screenshot of Gridania, Divinity's Reach, or Boralus — would a player pick
ours out as the amateur work? Would people choose to play here?

I am not a programmer. I will never read your code. I can only judge pictures,
so this project runs on one currency: **rendered images that have actually
been looked at**. Everything below exists to keep that currency honest.

## 2. What this file is, and is not

This file is the mission and the way of working. It is **not law about how
anything looks**, and it sits **outside the precedence chain**: it has no
authority over art, layout, lore, or review. Those live in the governed
documents in §3. If this file ever appears to disagree with one of them, the
governed document is right and this file is what needs updating.

Do not restate governed content here or anywhere else. A copied number is a
future contradiction.

## 4. Rules of evidence

Agents on this project fail in one specific way: they claim work is done,
placed, or beautiful without having looked; they invent lore to fill gaps; and
they drift from the style while insisting they haven't. Treat every claim
about the world as a courtroom claim — **it needs an exhibit**.

1. **A render is the only proof a thing exists.** If you have not opened and
   looked at an image of it, it is not built, not placed, and not finished.
   "Should look right" is a hope, not a state of the world.
2. **Where we are is written down, not remembered.** Project state lives in
   `review/validate.txt`, `review/reports/`, `docs/DECISIONS.md`, and
   `git log`. Read them at the start of every session (§5). Never trust
   memory, a summary, a previous session, or this file for state.
3. **A machine prover outranks any claim, including a render.** If a prover
   exists for a claim, run it: `tools/validate.py` (scale, palette,
   anachronisms, floating masses), `tools/check_walkable.mjs` (the town floods
   from spawn), `tools/check_client.mjs` (the real client boots clean),
   `tools/plan/townplan.py --check` (plan integrity and the arrival
   sightline), `tools/render/town.mjs` (whole-town renders and the perf gate
   against `review/perf-baseline.json`). If the prover fails, the claim is
   false no matter how the picture looks.
4. **Nobody grades their own homework.** A builder may not self-certify.
   ACCEPT exists only as a critic's written verdict in `review/reports/`. A
   critic never reads generator code before forming a visual judgement.
   `docs/REVIEW_PROTOCOL.md` is the law here.
5. **Re-review verifies each defect is resolved, not merely changed.** Read
   the prior report defect by defect against the new renders.
6. **Never invent lore, names, or history.** Canon lives in
   `docs/areas/hearthmere/WORLD_BIBLE.md` and `docs/world/`. A gap in canon is a question to
   record in `docs/DECISIONS.md`, not an invitation to fill.
7. **"Beautiful" is a scored verdict, not an adjective.** Praise without a
   report and scores per the review protocol means nothing; never write it.
8. **When documents and reality disagree, record it.** Do not silently pick a
   side. Append the discrepancy to `docs/DECISIONS.md` and resolve it in the
   open.

## 5. Session-start ritual

Every session that continues this work starts the same way, before building:

1. `git status` and `git log --oneline -15` — what is committed, what drifts.
2. Read the tail of `review/validate.txt` — the current machine verdict.
3. List `review/reports/` and read every report not superseded by a later
   ACCEPT on the same subject — the current human verdicts.
4. Read the tail of `docs/DECISIONS.md` — latest rulings, anything left open.
5. Only then pick work, by the order in §6.

If you inherit a dirty working tree, your first deliverable is committing it
in coherent, reviewable pieces (§7) — not building on quicksand.

This ritual is also the recovery procedure. Sessions end without warning —
usage limits (the 5-hour window, the weekly cap) cut work off mid-task, and
that is normal, not an emergency. A successor session that runs steps 1–5
needs nothing from the dead one: no memory, no summary, no luck. If the
ritual is ever not enough to resume, the dead session violated §7, and the
fix is tighter checkpoints, not more notes.

## 6. Current objective and priority order

The current objective is the **visual completion of Hearthmere**: every venue
placed in `content/town/hearthmere.json` exists as reviewed geometry with
authored collision, and the assembled town holds up as one place. No gameplay
systems. **No NPCs** — D-012 removed them and they stay removed until I say
otherwise. Interiors only as BUILD_DIRECTIVE §1 grants them.

Explicitly deferred, not forgotten: night and weather states (the rig stays
locked to 09:30 per Art Bible §4 until the town passes at it), walkable
interiors beyond the church, and anything that moves on the roads. Bringing
any of these into scope requires a decision entry, not a whim.

Work in this order. This is an **ordering, not a status board** — nothing
here says what is finished; only the artifacts in §4 rule 2 say that.

a. **The filler building kit reaches ACCEPT.** Every street is made of its
   houses; nothing sited among broken fillers can pass cohesion. (Reviews at
   `review/reports/building-kit-*.md`; under REJECT as of Aug 2026 — verify.)
b. **Church of Summoning, the altar, and the arrival frame.** The player
   spawns on the altar; the view out the west doors is the most important
   composition in the build (BUILD_DIRECTIVE §3, LOCKED). The town does not
   exist until its first two seconds do.
c. **Town wall, gates, towers, wall-walk.** The wall is the town's silhouette
   and every street's terminus.
d. **Remaining hero venues** — quay and crane, moot hall, watermill, bakery —
   per BUILD_DIRECTIVE §5 and their Town Plan rows.
e. **Secondary venues and remaining filler** until the plan has no empty plot.
f. **The furnishing and planting pass.** Street furniture (lanterns, benches,
   wells, troughs, hitching posts), trees, hedges and foliage, yard residue —
   judged street by street at the gameplay camera per Art Bible §7. A town
   with buildings but no furniture reads as a film set, not a place.
g. **Everything recorded as open in `docs/DECISIONS.md`** (terrain the plan
   promises, texel-density and atlas commitments, venues authored flat over
   falling ground).
h. **Cohesion pass and whole-town ACCEPT** — the definition of done in §8.

## 7. Orchestration

**Fan out, and keep the roles apart.** One builder sub-agent per venue: it
owns the generator, renders its own work at the locked 09:30 rig, and submits
READY_FOR_REVIEW with render paths. A separate critic sub-agent reviews each
submission under `docs/REVIEW_PROTOCOL.md` — images first, code never. When
venues pass, a cohesion critic judges the assembled town from the whole-town
render set. Iterate until ACCEPT. REVISE on a first submission is normal; do
not negotiate with the critic — fix the defects. At iteration 5, stop and
escalate to me: the renders, the report chain, and both positions in plain
language.

**Commit at reviewable checkpoints.** Commit after every review submission
and after every verdict is acted on, with messages naming the venue and the
report they answer. An uncommitted tree older than one iteration is itself a
defect: it breaks review diffing and puts weeks of work one crash from gone.
Never leave a session with more than one venue's work uncommitted.

**Assume the session can end without warning.** Usage limits — the 5-hour
window and the weekly cap — cut sessions off mid-task. Shape the work to
survive that: finish and commit one venue rather than starting three; never
fan out wider than can reach a committed checkpoint soon; commit small
completed units as they land instead of batching a day of work. When work
stops mid-venue, end the last commit message with a `NEXT:` line naming the
immediate next step — the successor session reads it in step 1 of the
ritual (§5), which is the resume procedure.

## 8. Definition of done

Hearthmere is done, and this standing order satisfied, when all of the
following are true at once:

- Every venue placed in `content/town/hearthmere.json` has geometry,
  collision, and a critic ACCEPT on file in `review/reports/`.
- The arrival frame has its own ACCEPT as a composition.
- A cohesion review of the assembled town stands at ACCEPT.
- `tools/validate.py` reports zero FAIL; `tools/check_walkable.mjs`,
  `tools/check_client.mjs`, and `tools/plan/townplan.py --check` pass;
  `tools/render/town.mjs` exits clean against `review/perf-baseline.json`.
- The whole-town render set (arrival frame, plan, aerials, street-level walk)
  has been generated fresh and actually looked at.

When every box is checked, do not declare victory. Assemble the render set
and bring it to me. I am the last critic.
