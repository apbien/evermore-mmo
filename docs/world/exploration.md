# Exploration

Exploration is one of the primary pillars of Evermore. Arkadion is not a series
of destinations to be consumed — it is a place to be understood.

Players are not simply moving through space. They are discovering locations,
studying environments, uncovering history, identifying patterns, learning
relationships between systems, and documenting the unknown.

Exploration is a form of progression. A player who understands Arkadion has
progressed, even if that knowledge does not immediately increase combat power.

## Exploration Philosophy

The world exists independently of the player. Players do not create the
mysteries of Arkadion; they uncover them. Ancient civilizations existed before
players arrived. Lost techniques were forgotten before players discovered them.
Hidden locations have meaning before anyone finds them.

The purpose of exploration is not simply to reveal content. It is to allow
players to build a relationship with the world.

## Exploration as Gameplay

Exploration should reward curiosity, not only completion. Players should be
encouraged to ask: *Why is this here? What happened in this place? What happens
if I interact with this? What connects these discoveries together?*

## Exploration Rewards

Exploration may provide new locations, historical knowledge, unique resources,
hidden quests, rare creatures, forgotten techniques, new Skills, new Arts,
reputation, titles, achievements, and social recognition.

Not every discovery needs to provide immediate power. Some discoveries should
exist because they deepen understanding.

## Exploration Depth

Not all discoveries are equal. A player may encounter:

**Surface Discovery** — the player learns that something exists. (A ruined tower
is found.)

**Investigative Discovery** — the player learns information about the discovery.
(The tower belonged to an ancient civilization.)

**Complete Understanding** — the player understands the purpose, history, and
significance. (Why the civilization built the tower, and how its technology
worked.)

The deepest discoveries should require observation, experimentation, and
persistence.

## Fog of War

The world map begins incomplete. Players should not immediately know every
location, settlement, resource, creature, or point of interest. Unknown regions
create curiosity.

Fog of War exists to support discovery, not to artificially hide information. The
purpose is not "players cannot know this" but "players have not yet experienced
this."

## Revealing the World

Exploration gradually reveals terrain, landmarks, settlements, points of
interest, environmental changes, and historical information. But revealing a
location does not mean understanding it — a location may remain partially unknown
even after being discovered.

## Regional Knowledge

Regions have their own levels of understanding. A player may know where a forest
is without knowing what creatures live there, what resources exist, what dangers
exist, or what history occurred there.

Cartography represents knowledge, not just visibility.
