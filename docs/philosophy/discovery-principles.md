# Discovery Principles

Discovery is Evermore's primary progression path. This document defines how
discovery works as a philosophy — the systems that implement it live in
`../systems/unlocks-and-discovery.md`.

## The World Should Be Discoverable, Not Solvable

Players will create wikis, share discoveries, publish guides, optimize systems,
and document mechanics. This is not something to prevent. The goal is not
permanent secrecy.

The goal is systems where knowledge *expands possibilities* rather than reducing
the game to a checklist. Knowing the answer should create new questions. A player
discovering a technique should not mean the journey is over — it should reveal
deeper possibilities.

## Discovery Is Gameplay

Players are rewarded for exploring unknown regions, investigating rumors,
experimenting with systems, studying creatures, interacting with the
environment, and solving mysteries.

The core player question should frequently be: *"What happens if I try this?"*

Discovery is not optional lore. It directly influences character progression,
reputation, crafting, combat, exploration, and social value.

## Prestige Over Exclusivity

Evermore values being first. It does not value permanent ownership of
information.

The first player to discover something receives recognition through historical
records, titles, achievements, reputation, and world recognition. But
discoveries eventually become part of the community. A player who discovers a
technique creates history; a player who learns it continues the tradition.

Knowledge spreads. Prestige remains.

## On Secrecy and Datamining

Evermore protects the **journey** of revelation, not the **fact** of it.

The complete unlock graph is never client-readable, so discovery happens through
play and community over time rather than through datamining in a day. But
individual discoveries are *meant* to spread and become shared knowledge. This is
not a failure state — it is the design.

The defense against a discovered secret losing its value is not hiding it
harder. It is designing secrets that survive being known: a leaked trigger
should be an *invitation*, not a *spoiler*. Knowing that a technique requires
certain conditions does not perform those conditions for you. See
`../systems/unlocks-and-discovery.md` for the repeatable-vs-consumable secret
classification that makes this concrete.
