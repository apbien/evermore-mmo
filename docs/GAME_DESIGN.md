# Purpose

This document defines the foundational philosophy of Evermore.

It establishes the principles that guide:

- worldbuilding
- progression
- discovery
- skills
- player identity
- long-term MMO design

Individual systems should expand upon this document.

This document defines the "why" behind the game.

---

# High Concept

Evermore is a persistent MMORPG centered around discovery, mastery, and personal identity.

Players grow by understanding the world.

Progression is not defined only by:

- level
- equipment
- damage output
- optimization

A player's identity is shaped by:

- what they discover
- what they learn
- how they specialize
- how they express their abilities

Arkadion should always contain possibilities waiting to be uncovered.

---

# Core Philosophy

## Understanding Is Progression

Information alone is not mastery.

A player can know a skill exists without understanding how to use it.

Evermore rewards:

- experimentation
- observation
- application
- specialization

A guide can reveal possibilities.

It cannot replace mastery.

---

## The World Should Be Discoverable, Not Solvable

Evermore accepts that players will:

- create wikis
- share discoveries
- optimize systems
- publish guides

The goal is not to prevent information sharing.

The goal is to ensure that information does not remove the depth of the world.

Knowledge should open possibilities, not reduce the game to a checklist.

---

## Discovery Is Gameplay

Discovery is a primary progression path.

Players should be rewarded for:

- exploring unknown areas
- investigating rumors
- experimenting with systems
- interacting with the world
- noticing unusual patterns

The game should encourage the question:

> "What happens if I try this?"

# World Philosophy

Arkadion is an ancient world that exists independently of the player.

Players uncover:

- forgotten histories
- lost techniques
- hidden locations
- unusual creatures
- unexplained phenomena

The player is an explorer of the world, not the creator of it.

---

# Civilization

Major settlements are designated as Havens.

Each Haven has:

- a numerical designation
- a historical name

Examples:

- Haven I: Hearth
- Haven II: Brookmere
- Haven X: Everlight

The numbering system provides practical communication.

The historical name provides identity and lore.

---

# Everlight

Everlight is the Grand Capital of Arkadion.

It represents the center of civilization and the known world.

---

# Character Progression

Character growth is open-ended.

Players are not expected to follow identical progression paths.

Two players with similar playtime should be capable of becoming fundamentally different.

Character identity comes from accumulated choices.

---

# Skill System

Skills represent knowledge gained from the world.

A skill answers:

> "What can this character do?"

Skills are not merely abilities.

They represent understanding.
