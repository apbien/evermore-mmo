# Purpose

This document defines the permanent design philosophy of Evermore. It is intended to remain stable while individual mechanics evolve in separate design documents.

## Vision

Evermore is a persistent MMORPG about **understanding**.

Discovery begins progression.
Understanding creates mastery.
Mastery creates identity.

The game can be documented, but it cannot be exhausted.

# Core Philosophy

## Understanding Is Progression

Information alone is not progression. A guide can reveal possibilities but cannot replace experimentation, practice, and mastery.

## The World Should Be Discoverable, Not Solvable

Players will create guides, wikis, and communities. This is encouraged. Systems should continue producing meaningful decisions even after information is widely known.

## Discovery Is Gameplay

Exploration, experimentation, observation, investigation, and interaction are primary gameplay loops.

## Prestige Over Exclusivity

First discoverers earn permanent historical recognition, but knowledge belongs to the community once shared.

# World

Game: Evermore

World: Arkadion

Arkadion is an ancient world that predates the player. Players uncover forgotten histories, hidden places, unusual creatures, and lost techniques rather than creating the world's history.

## Civilization

Major settlements are Havens with both numerical designations and historical names (e.g. Haven I: Hearth, Haven X: Everlight).

Everlight is the Grand Capital and symbolic center of civilization.

# Character Identity

Evermore avoids rigid permanent classes.

Identity emerges from:

- Skills learned
- Arts practiced
- Disciplines pursued
- Equipment choices
- Discoveries made
- Reputation earned

Two equally experienced players should be capable of becoming fundamentally different.

# Progression

Progress is measured through understanding rather than simple numbers.

Primary progression pillars:

- Exploration
- Skills
- Arts
- Disciplines
- Mastery
- Reputation
- Knowledge

# Skills

Skills represent learned capability.

Each skill contains:

- Rarity
- Rank
- Art expression
- Mastery

Rarity reflects significance and discovery, not absolute power.

# Arts

Arts modify how a skill is expressed.

A skill defines capability.

An Art defines expression.

The same skill can create vastly different playstyles through different Arts.

# Disciplines

Disciplines are long-term areas of specialization that shape a character's identity across combat, exploration, crafting, and research.

# Exploration & Cartography

The world is partially unknown.

Exploration rewards curiosity rather than map completion alone.

Cartography documents regions, discoveries, expeditions, and history.

# Information Economy

Knowledge is valuable.

Players may discover, teach, trade, document, and preserve knowledge.

# Social Philosophy

Players become known for different contributions:

- Explorers
- Researchers
- Artisans
- Historians
- Combat specialists
- Collectors
- Teachers

# Design Principles

Future systems should encourage:

- Curiosity
- Experimentation
- Mastery
- Exploration
- Specialization
- Emergent player stories

Avoid systems where one optimal path invalidates creativity or discovery.
